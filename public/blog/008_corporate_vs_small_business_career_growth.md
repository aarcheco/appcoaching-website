<p>Corporate vs small business? If you want your career to grow fast, an AI-forward small business usually wins. You own whole functions instead of one slice, you use new tools the week they come out, and your work changes decisions straight away. For me, months in a small team delivered what would have been years in corporate.</p>

<h2 id="big-4-to-small-team">From Big 4 to Small Team</h2>

<p>My CV reads like a tour of Australian corporate: PwC, Flight Centre, Telstra, RACQ, Aveo. I collected job titles the way other kids collected footy cards.</p>

<p>Every role taught me something. But every role was also a slice: accounts payable here, a budget model there, one report for one team.</p>

<p>Then I joined an AI-forward small business. Within months I'd taken on far more than my job title said, and learned more than in any single corporate year. <strong>Months in a small team, years of corporate growth.</strong> Sleep, sadly, not included (new dad, different story).</p>

<h2 id="why-now">Why Now</h2>

<p>Small businesses aren't waiting for permission to use AI.</p>

<ul>
  <li><a href="https://ai.gov.au/news-and-insights/blog/ai-adoption-insights-december-2025-february-2026" style="color: #1f3a7d; text-decoration: underline; text-decoration-color: #00FF00; text-decoration-thickness: 2px; font-weight: 600;">43% of Australian SMEs reported some level of AI adoption</a> between December 2025 and February 2026, and broad use across multiple parts of the business hit a seven-month high.</li>
  <li>Employers want the skills to match. In February 2026, <a href="https://hiringlab.indeed.com/au/blog/2026/04/01/nothing-artificial-about-australian-ai-adoption/" style="color: #1f3a7d; text-decoration: underline; text-decoration-color: #00FF00; text-decoration-thickness: 2px; font-weight: 600;">6.2% of Australian job ads on Indeed mentioned AI, up from 3.3% a year earlier</a>.</li>
</ul>

<p>The skills employers are starting to ask for are <strong>the ones a small, AI-forward team lets you practise every day</strong>.</p>

<h2 id="four-accelerators">Corporate vs Small Business: Four Things That Sped Up My Growth</h2>

<ol>
  <li>Owning the whole function. No more "that's another team's job". When you own month-end, forecasting and reporting, you see how it all connects, and you learn fast because nobody else is going to fix it.</li>
  <li>New tech every month. AI agents, connecting AI to accounting systems, databases, automation. In a small team, you try it this week and ship it next week. That's how I ended up with a finance dashboard built by Claude in minutes, which I wrote about in <a href="/blog/power-bi-took-me-months-claude-and-supabase-took-minutes" style="color: #1f3a7d; text-decoration: underline; text-decoration-color: #00FF00; text-decoration-thickness: 2px; font-weight: 600;">Power BI Took Me Months. Claude and Supabase Took Minutes.</a></li>
  <li>A direct line to the decision-maker. Your analysis lands with the person deciding, the same day. You find out fast what matters and what was a waste of a spreadsheet.</li>
  <li>Coaching and communicating. In a small business you explain numbers to non-finance people constantly. It's <strong>the most underrated finance skill there is</strong>, and you get daily reps.</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="four-brakes">The Four Brakes in Corporate</h2>

<ol>
  <li>Approvals and IT tickets. Weeks to get access to a dataset or a new tool. I've submitted access requests that took longer than my 2024 Euro trip, and that trip had its own spreadsheet.</li>
  <li>A narrow role. The same slice of work, month after month, with little sight of the rest.</li>
  <li>Locked-down tools. Approved software only, and AI often blocked entirely. Hard to learn tools you're not allowed to open.</li>
  <li>Slow change. A good idea becomes a working group, then a pilot, then a steering committee. By then, the tool has had three new versions.</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="find-the-role">How to Find an AI-Forward Small Business Role</h2>

<ol>
  <li>Read job ads for signals. Look for named AI tools, words like "own" and "build", and roles reporting to a founder or director.</li>
  <li>Ask the right interview questions. "What AI tools does the team use day to day?" "Who will I report to?" "What could I own in six months?"</li>
  <li>Build one AI project you can show. A dashboard, an automation, a prompt kit. <strong>Proof beats a job title.</strong></li>
  <li>Score the ad before you apply, using the prompt below.</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="thrive-small-team">What It Takes to Thrive in a Small Team</h2>

<p>The speed is real, but it doesn't happen to you. It happens because you lean into it. The people who grow fastest in small teams tend to share a few habits:</p>

<ul>
  <li>They start before they're ready. Nobody hands you a training plan, so you learn the tool while you use it, and ask AI to explain what you don't get.</li>
  <li>They write things down. When you own a whole process, a one-page guide for it is what lets you hand it over and move on to the next thing.</li>
  <li>They're comfortable with "good enough for now". Version one ships this week; version two ships next week.</li>
  <li>They show their work. A short weekly update to the decision-maker turns quiet effort into visible progress.</li>
</ul>

<p>If you read that list and thought "yes, finally", you'll probably love it. If you thought "that sounds exhausting", that's worth knowing before you jump.</p>

<h2 id="staying-in-corporate">Staying in Corporate for Now? Grow Anyway</h2>

<p>Not everyone can switch jobs this year, and that's fine. You can still borrow the small-business playbook:</p>

<ol>
  <li>Pick one manual process in your team and automate it, even if it's just a better spreadsheet or a reusable AI prompt.</li>
  <li>Ask which AI tools are approved, and become the person who actually knows how to use them well.</li>
  <li>Volunteer to explain the numbers. Offer to walk a non-finance manager through a report each month.</li>
  <li>Build one project outside work you can show in an interview, like the dashboard in my Power BI post.</li>
</ol>

<p>None of these need permission from a steering committee, and every one of them builds proof for your next role.</p>
