<p>What's your real job in finance? It isn't to make the numbers balance. It's to help the organisation be <strong>more effective and more efficient</strong>. Effective means working on what moves the numbers that matter. Efficient means doing that work with the least time, money and errors, again and again. Right now, AI is the biggest new tool for doing both. But it only helps if you understand what the work does for the business.</p>

<h2 id="two-words">Effective and Efficient: What Leaders Care About</h2>

<p>Every leader I've worked for, at PwC clients, Flight Centre, Aveo, Coronis and a charity, cared about the same two things:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Effectiveness</strong> is impact. Is this work moving a big line on the P&amp;L or balance sheet, or nothing at all?</li>
  <li><strong>Efficiency</strong> is doing that work with the least time, money and mistakes, so the business hits its best result every week, not just once.</li>
</ul>

<p>It doesn't matter if the organisation is listed, family-owned or a not-for-profit. At yourtown, the charity behind Kids Helpline, a stronger surplus means more counsellors answering more calls, which is why the regulator says <a href="https://www.acnc.gov.au/node/983" style="color: #1f3a7d; text-decoration: underline; text-decoration-color: #00FF00; text-decoration-thickness: 2px; font-weight: 600;">generating a surplus is generally considered good practice for charities</a>. Nobody gets a dividend, but everyone cares about results.</p>

<p>That's also why "continuous improvement" turns up in almost every finance job ad. Leaders need people in the engine room who keep making the business more effective and more efficient. As a grad, I thought my job was to make things balance. It took me an embarrassingly long time to realise the business didn't much care if the numbers balanced if they balanced badly.</p>

<h2 id="find-impact">Effective: Find the Work That Moves the Numbers</h2>

<p>Start with the financials and work backwards. Every big line on the P&amp;L has drivers:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Income drivers:</strong> sales volume, price, billable or care hours, how much of each staff member's time actually earns revenue.</li>
  <li><strong>Cost drivers:</strong> staff hours and wages, suppliers, rework, late fees, systems.</li>
</ul>

<p>Behind each driver sit the operational, sales and staffing processes, and the KPIs that measure them. Rank them by the size of the line they move. The processes behind the biggest lines are where effectiveness lives. The ones that move very little, or nothing, are candidates to simplify or stop.</p>

<p>At Flight Centre, store leaders would ask me, "why does my P&amp;L look like this?" The useful answer was never a debit and a credit. It was which drivers moved their profit. Once they saw that, they changed how they ran the store. Nobody wins a grand final by staring at the scoreboard. (Everton fans have had plenty of practice.)</p>

<h2 id="who-cares">Effective: Ask Who Cares</h2>

<p>Before you improve any process, ask one blunt question: who uses this, and what decision does it change?</p>

<p>At Flight Centre I found a team in the marketing division doing a monthly bank reconciliation. I asked who looked at the bank account. They named an executive. So I asked the executive. They'd never heard of it. A team was carefully reconciling a bank account that should have been closed years earlier.</p>

<p>Beautifully reconciled, to the cent, for nobody. Peak efficiency, zero effectiveness. Big organisations are full of these: reports with no reader, approvals nobody remembers adding, outputs no executive has ever seen. Every one costs labour hours and wages, straight off the bottom line.</p>

<h2 id="efficient">Efficient: Do the Important Work Faster and Right</h2>

<p>Once you know which work matters, make it efficient. That cuts costs and, just as often, grows income.</p>

<p><strong>Cutting cost:</strong> at Coronis I inherited the classics: reporting rebuilt by hand every period, the same data typed into more than one system, approvals that sat for days, reports nobody read and paper processes that should have been digital years ago. Fixing them freed up more than 40% of my capacity.</p>

<p><strong>Growing income:</strong> at Aveo, income came from care staff delivering care hours. We tracked utilisation (care hours delivered against hours paid), made it visible to managers, and fixed rostering and scheduling so more of each shift went to actual care. Same team, more care delivered, more income. Trades and construction work the same way: slow quoting, paperwork and chasing materials mean fewer billable hours, so a better process means more revenue.</p>

<p>There's plenty of room to improve. One survey found <a href="https://www.corpay.com/resources/blog/where-finance-time-disappears" style="color: #1f3a7d; text-decoration: underline; text-decoration-color: #00FF00; text-decoration-thickness: 2px; font-weight: 600;">86% of finance teams spend six or more hours per person, per week on admin</a>, and another found <a href="https://www.cpapracticeadvisor.com/2026/08/11/study-reveals-gap-between-the-ai-ambitions-of-accounting-teams-and-their-ability-to-execute/188332/" style="color: #1f3a7d; text-decoration: underline; text-decoration-color: #00FF00; text-decoration-thickness: 2px; font-weight: 600;">six in ten accountants spend 40% or more of their time on reconciliations, data entry and other busy work</a>.</p>

<h2 id="ai">Then Excel, Now AI</h2>

<p>For most of my career, my efficiency engine was Excel. Pivot tables, Power Query and built-in checks and balances made my journals and reports faster, more accurate and easier for the next person to run. It's how I ended up as <a href="/blog/25-common-excel-mistakes-i-fixed-for-years-and-how-i-became-mr-excel-at-flight-centre" style="color: #1f3a7d; text-decoration: underline; text-decoration-color: #00FF00; text-decoration-thickness: 2px; font-weight: 600;">"Mr Excel" at Flight Centre</a>.</p>

<p>AI tools like Claude do that same job, and far more:</p>

<ul>
  <li><strong>Spreadsheets:</strong> formulas, data cleaning and reconciliation checks in minutes.</li>
  <li><strong>Emails:</strong> follow-ups, explanations and chasers drafted in seconds.</li>
  <li><strong>Reports and dashboards:</strong> built in an afternoon, not a month. I wrote about <a href="/blog/power-bi-took-me-months-claude-and-supabase-took-minutes" style="color: #1f3a7d; text-decoration: underline; text-decoration-color: #00FF00; text-decoration-thickness: 2px; font-weight: 600;">a dashboard that took me months in Power BI and minutes with AI</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>AI agents:</strong> whole processes, like bank recs, invoice reminders and bill entry, running on a schedule and handing a person a short review list. I <a href="/blog/your-bookkeepers-on-holiday-your-ai-agents-arent-xero-over-the-christmas-shutdown" style="color: #1f3a7d; text-decoration: underline; text-decoration-color: #00FF00; text-decoration-thickness: 2px; font-weight: 600;">tested this in Xero</a>.</li>
  <li><strong>Insight:</strong> the "so what" in front of executives faster, so they make effective decisions faster.</li>
</ul>

<p>It also cuts human error. Manual processes are where mistakes creep in: <a href="https://insurance-edge.net/2026/07/10/the-manual-tax-is-the-cost-no-finance-leader-has-budgeted-for/" style="color: #1f3a7d; text-decoration: underline; text-decoration-color: #00FF00; text-decoration-thickness: 2px; font-weight: 600;">85% of finance leaders in one survey said their current payment processes increase the risk of error, fraud or off-policy spend</a>. Automation with the right checks makes the result more consistent every time, which is efficiency and effectiveness together.</p>

<p>This is new technology, and businesses will adopt it because it makes them more effective and efficient. The finance people who learn it now will be the ones leading that change instead of catching up to it. Excel and I had a great run, and we're still friends. I'm just seeing other tools now.</p>

<h2 id="judgement">AI Won't Do Your Thinking</h2>

<p>Here's the catch. An AI agent will happily reconcile that dead marketing bank account every month, faster and more accurately than any human ever did. That's an efficient waste.</p>

<p>AI makes a process faster. It doesn't tell you whether the process matters. That's still your job as a finance professional: understand which drivers it moves, who uses the output, and what the automated version does to the business's commercial performance. Then check the agent's work is accurate. The person who understands both the tech and the commercial impact is the one leaders want.</p>

<h2 id="day-one">How to Think Like This From Day One</h2>

<ol>
  <li><strong>Learn the drivers.</strong> What moves the biggest income and cost lines? Ask. Nobody minds a grad asking that.</li>
  <li><strong>Ask who cares.</strong> For every process you touch: who uses the output, and what decision does it change? No answer, no process.</li>
  <li><strong>Rate it twice.</strong> How effective is it (size of impact) and how efficient is it (time, money and errors)?</li>
  <li><strong>Learn AI on a real task.</strong> Pick one high-impact, inefficient process and use Claude or another AI tool to speed it up. Learning on real work beats any course.</li>
  <li><strong>Measure the result.</strong> Hours saved, dollars saved, errors avoided, income unlocked. "This took 6 hours a week; now it takes one" gets attention.</li>
  <li><strong>Then the next one.</strong> That's continuous improvement. My next post covers <a href="/blog/which-process-should-you-fix-first-follow-the-time-and-the-money" style="color: #1f3a7d; text-decoration: underline; text-decoration-color: #00FF00; text-decoration-thickness: 2px; font-weight: 600;">how to pick which process to fix first</a>.</li>
</ol>

<h2 id="your-prompt">Find Where Your Work Has Impact</h2>

<p>This prompt interviews you about your organisation's drivers and your week, rates every task for effectiveness and efficiency, and suggests where AI could help. It's a slightly confronting exercise. Mine would have shown a lot of very well-formatted nothing in my grad year.</p>

<strong>📋 PAUL's Prompt (Profit Line-of-Sight)</strong>
<p>Paste it into Claude, ChatGPT or Gemini and answer its questions honestly.</p>

<details style="background: #ffffff; border: 1px solid #e5e7eb; border-radius: 8px; padding: 1.5rem; margin: 1rem 0;">
  <summary style="cursor: pointer; font-weight: 700; color: #1f3a7d;">📋 See Full Prompt (Click to Expand)</summary>
  <p class="paul-prompt">You're an experienced finance business partner. Help me see which parts of my work are effective (they move the numbers that matter) and which are just efficient at being pointless.

First, interview me one question at a time:
1. What does my organisation do, and how does it make money? (If it's a not-for-profit: where does its funding come from, and what does a surplus pay for?)
2. What are the biggest income and cost lines on our P&L, and what drives them (e.g. sales volume, price, billable or care hours, staff hours, supplier costs)?
3. What's my role and team?
4. List every task I do in a typical week and month, roughly how long each takes, and who uses the output.

Then build me a table with these columns: task, hours per month, who uses the output and for what decision, which P&L or balance sheet line it affects, size of impact (high, medium, low, none), and how efficiently it's done now (good, could be better, wasteful).

After the table:
1. My 3 most effective tasks: the ones that move the biggest lines.
2. My 3 biggest wastes: tasks nobody uses, or that take far more time than their impact deserves, with the hours per month they cost.
3. Any task where nobody can name who uses the output. Tell me to ask that question before I do it again.
4. For each waste, suggest a fix (stop, simplify, automate or hand off). Where AI could help, say how (e.g. Claude for analysis and drafting, or an AI agent running it on a schedule), what could go wrong, and what a person should still check.
5. 3 questions I could ask my manager to find out which numbers they care about most right now.

Keep it plain English. Be honest if a task adds nothing.</p>
</details>
